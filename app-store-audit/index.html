    What the audit checks.

    Eleven things that decide whether a store visit becomes an install, weighted by how much they usually move. Each one is something you can fix this week.

    01 · Search

    App name

    The name is the strongest ranking field on the App Store and it has 30 characters. A name that is only the brand leaves search traffic unclaimed. Brand first, then what the app does after a dash.

    02 · Search

    Languages

    Every localized store page gets its own name, subtitle, keyword field and screenshots. Apple supports 50 App Store localizations, and several of them, such as Spanish (Mexico) and Portuguese (Brazil), also appear in the US store. Which languages to add first →

    03 · Store page

    iPhone screenshots

    You get up to 10 per device size. Most visitors never scroll past the first three, so those carry the pitch: the outcome, the feature people pay for, and proof. Every size Apple accepts →

    04 · Store page

    iPad gallery

    If your app is built for iPad, iPad shoppers should see iPad screenshots. Apple asks for the 13-inch size and scales it down for smaller iPads.

    05 · Store page

    Description

    The App Store doesn't rank on it, but the people who tap “more” are close to installing, and Google indexes your App Store web page. Only about three lines show before the fold.

    06 · Trust

    Star rating

    Stars sit next to your icon in every search result and ad. Below 4.5, people start comparing. The fix is usually one recurring complaint, then a better-timed review prompt.

    07 · Trust

    Number of ratings

    A 4.8 from 40 people is not the same as a 4.6 from 40,000. Apple's in-app rating prompt can show up to three times a year per user. Ask right after a win, never on launch.

    08 · Freshness

    Last update

    Visitors check the date. Months without a release make an app look abandoned next to active competitors, and usually mean nobody is testing the store page either.

    09 · Freshness

    Release notes

    They show on your page and in every user's update list. “Bug fixes and improvements” wastes a free message to your whole audience.

    10 · Money

    Pricing model

    A price tag stops most people before they try the app. For many apps, a free download with a trial and an in-app purchase earns more per visitor than paid upfront.

    11 · Money

    Download size

    Above 200 MB, iPhones on cellular ask before downloading by default, and some people walk away. On-demand resources keep the first download small.

    Each check is Good, Improve or Fix first, and counts in proportion to its weight. Facts like category and minimum iOS are shown but not scored.

    Audit questions.

    Is the App Store audit really free?

    Yes. No signup, no email, no limit. It runs in your browser on Apple's public data. If you like what you see, you can apply for a growth plan, but you don't have to.

    What data does the audit use?

    Only what Apple publishes for every app: the name, supported languages, screenshots, ratings, version history, release notes, description, size and price from the public App Store listing. We don't need access to your App Store Connect account.

    Does it work for Google Play apps?

    Not yet. Google doesn't offer a comparable public data feed, so we review Google Play listings by hand. Apply with your Play link and we'll send the audit with your growth plan.

    Why doesn't it check my subtitle and keywords?

    Apple doesn't expose the subtitle or the hidden keyword field in its public data. Those are two of the most important search fields, so we review them by hand during a growth plan.

    Which country's App Store does it read?

    The US storefront by default. Paste a link that includes a country code, like apps.apple.com/gb/app/…, to audit another storefront.

    How often should I run it?

    After every store page change and at least once a month. Ratings, update age and release notes move all the time, and the report link always shows the current state of your page.
